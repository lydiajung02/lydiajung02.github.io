Sentiment Trend Modeling of ChatGPT Tweets

An NLP analysis of public sentiment toward ChatGPT over time, built from a large volume of tweets — cleaned, language-filtered, scored for sentiment, and tracked as a trend rather than a single snapshot.

This page summarizes an academic/independent project referenced on my resume. Full code and the underlying dataset live in my GitHub — this write-up focuses on the reasoning and NLP pipeline behind it.

How Did I Do This?

  1. 1. Data collection Worked with a large volume of ChatGPT-related tweets, which meant the first real problem wasn't sentiment scoring — it was volume and noise: duplicate retweets, non-English text, and bot-like spam all needed to be filtered before any analysis was meaningful.
  2. 2. Preprocessing Used langdetect to filter to English-language tweets specifically, since sentiment models trained on English text give unreliable scores on other languages rather than simply failing loudly. Standard NLP cleaning followed: stripping URLs/mentions/hashtags, lowercasing, and removing boilerplate retweet text.
  3. 3. Exploratory text analysis Used wordcloud alongside frequency counts to sanity-check what people were actually talking about before trusting any sentiment number — this surfaced topic shifts (e.g. spikes tied to specific ChatGPT feature releases or news cycles) that later explained some of the sentiment swings.
  4. 4. Sentiment scoring & trend modeling Scored each cleaned tweet for sentiment and aggregated into time-based cohorts rather than looking at individual tweets, since single-tweet sentiment is noisy but the trend across thousands of tweets per period is stable enough to interpret.
  5. 5. Statistical comparison Used comparative analysis and A/B-testing-style comparisons between early and later cohorts to confirm the apparent decline in positive sentiment wasn't just noise, rather than eyeballing the trend line alone.
