Sentiment Analysis of ChatGPT Tweets

Executive Summary

Using Python, I pulled about 224,000 tweets mentioning ChatGPT from its first three months after launch (Nov 30, 2022 to Feb 24, 2023) and labeled their sentiment with VADER. After finding that positive tweets outnumbered negative ones about 3.4 to 1, and that positive sentiment dipped in early 2023, I conclude that:

  1. The launch drew more enthusiasm than concern, with positive tweets far outnumbering negative ones (56% vs 16.5%).
  2. The decline in positive sentiment (57.1% to 55.2%, Jan to Feb 2023) has a possible correlation to the debate that arose over people's concerns about AI security.

Significance of This Project

Public reception is essential to how well OpenAI can grow ChatGPT, since adoption depends on whether people welcome or reject it. Immediately following the November 2022 launch: how can we quantify public opinion on Twitter to gauge how well ChatGPT is being received, track how that opinion changes over time, and find the events that line up with any shifts in sentiment?

Methodology

  1. Clean & detect language Cleaned the raw tweet data with Python and detected each tweet's language with langdetect.
  2. Preprocess & explore Preprocessed the English tweets and built a word cloud of the most frequent words to sanity-check the topic before scoring sentiment.
  3. Label sentiment & track over time Labeled sentiment with VADER and tracked it over time, then researched news from the period to look for events that line up with any shifts.
  4. Test engagement by sentiment Ran a permutation A/B test to check whether positive and negative tweets get different likes, after filtering out zero-engagement tweets and outliers.

Skills

Python: Pandas to clean and aggregate the data, langdetect to detect language, VADER to label sentiment, NumPy to run the permutation test

Visualization: Plotly, Seaborn, Matplotlib to show sentiment over time

Statistics: Permutation A/B test (5,000 simulations, p < 0.05 cutoff) on likes, with IQR outlier removal

SQL: Querying the tweet CSV

Results & Business Recommendation

Tracking tweets about ChatGPT gives insight into early public opinion. Attention grew fast, with the daily tweet rate rising about 78% from December to February. Positive tweets outnumbered negative ones about 3.4 to 1, and that ratio remained consistent even as volume grew. The one visible dip came from January to February 2023, when positive sentiment fell from 57.1% to 55.2%, and negative rose from 15.9% to 16.9%. This timing lines up with the rise of AI security bias debate, showing a possible correlation. Negative and positive tweets also got similar likes (p = 0.16), so negativity wasn't amplified.

Because opinion stayed strongly positive, and the only dip coincided with a public controversy, I read the launch as broadly well received, with sentiment sensitive to news and debate. I offer the following insights:

  1. ChatGPT's early reception was strong enough to support growth, since enthusiasm clearly outweighed concern.
  2. Shifts in sentiment may reflect a wider public debate and not opinions of the product itself.
  3. The dip in positive sentiment was small, so it is not strong enough to show that opinion turned against ChatGPT.

Next Steps

  1. Validate sentiment against a hand-labeled sample, or compare with a model built for social media text.
  2. Extend the time range to see whether the trend holds beyond the launch period.
  3. Compare with non-ChatGPT tweets to establish a baseline for general Twitter sentiment.
  4. Analyze sentiment daily around key events to test the link more closely.
