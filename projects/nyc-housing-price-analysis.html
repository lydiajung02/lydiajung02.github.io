NYC Housing Price Analysis

An exploratory and regression analysis of NYC residential sales data, aimed at answering one question for a hypothetical buyer-side client: which features actually move price, and which are noise?

How Did I Do This?

  1. 1. Data sourcing & cleaning Started from a NYC property sales extract (borough, neighborhood, square footage, sale price, building age, year built). Dropped $0 and nominal-dollar sales (family transfers, not real market transactions) and winsorized the top/bottom 1% of price-per-sqft to limit outlier influence.
  2. 2. Exploratory analysis Price distributions differ enormously by borough, so I looked at price-per-square-foot rather than raw price throughout, and used a correlation matrix to check which numeric features were worth including before modeling.
  3. 3. Feature engineering Derived price-per-sqft, building age (sale year − year built), and an approximate distance-to-nearest-subway-stop feature, since proximity to transit is a well-known price driver in NYC that raw address doesn't capture directly.
  4. 4. Modeling Fit a multiple linear regression (sqft, borough, building age, subway distance) rather than jumping straight to a black-box model, since the goal was explaining drivers, not just predicting. Checked variance inflation factors (VIF) to confirm borough and neighborhood weren't dangerously collinear before trusting the coefficients.
  5. 5. Insights & caveats Square footage and borough explained most of the variance (R² 0.78 on a held-out test set); subway distance mattered but far less than expected once borough was controlled for. Flagged clearly that this is observational data — the model explains association, not causal effect of any single renovation or feature.
