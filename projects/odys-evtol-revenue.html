eVTOL Revenue Impact Analysis

Executive Summary

Using Python, our team analyzed travel and flight data around six U.S. hub airports to estimate how much yearly revenue airlines could add by flying Odys eVTOL aircraft. After finding that the Midwest hubs have far more feeder airports than the rest, we conclude that:

  1. The Midwest is the best place to deploy first. Delta could grow annual revenue by 2.4% at MSP and DTW, and American by 1.6% at ORD.
  2. Nearly all of that revenue, about 97%, comes from bringing in flyers from smaller nearby airports, not from drivers who live near the hub.

Significance of This Project

Odys has a limited number of aircraft, so where it deploys first decides how much it earns and how attractive it is to airlines. Using flight and travel data, how can we estimate the revenue each airline could gain at specific hubs, find where Odys should start, and see what that revenue depends on?

Methodology

  1. Assign catchment airports We matched each of the six hubs with the smaller airports that feed it, such as BOI, GEG and PDX for SEA.
  2. Filter the flyers We counted local drivers who would get there faster by eVTOL than by car, and flyers at smaller airports who would do better flying to the hub.
  3. Estimate revenue Using fare assumptions of $117 per local flyer and $1,500 per feeder flyer, we multiplied by the flyer counts, then divided by each airline's yearly revenue to get the percent increase.
  4. Compare regions We ran the estimate separately for the Pacific Northwest, Midwest and Texas hubs so that one region's result would not hide another's.

Skills

Python: Pandas to handle the StreetLight data, and helper functions for total revenue, driver counts, drivers by income group and the most popular census tracts, reusable for other hubs and airlines

Geospatial: Catchment airport assignment and drive times from census tracts to airports

Results & Business Recommendation

Estimating revenue at six hubs shows where Odys would matter most. The Midwest came out ahead. ORD could add $729M a year for American, a 1.6% increase, and DTW and MSP could add $771M and $359M for Delta, a 2.4% increase together. The Pacific Northwest and Texas hubs added much less, between 0.17% and 0.29% of airline revenue. Feeder flyers drove the result, since each is worth $1,500 against $117 for a local driver, and ORD and DTW have 12 and 11 feeder airports against 3 for SEA.

Because the Midwest lead comes from how many airports feed its hubs, we read it as the strongest place to start, though the numbers rest on the fares used. We offer the following insights:

  1. Odys should deploy first at ORD, DTW and MSP, where the most flyers and the most revenue are.
  2. The business case depends on feeding hubs from smaller airports, so Odys should be offered as a regional feeder service, not a local shuttle.
  3. The estimates assume every eligible flyer switches and that fares hold, and feeder lists overlap between hubs, so some flyers may be counted twice. The true figure is likely lower.

Next Steps

  1. Check the $117 and $1,500 fares against real ticket prices.
  2. Remove flyers counted at more than one hub.
  3. Test how revenue changes if only a share of flyers switch, instead of all of them.
  4. Add more hubs and airlines using the same helper functions.
