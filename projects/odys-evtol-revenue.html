Odys eVTOL Revenue Strategy

A consulting analysis for Odys Aviation and Delta/American Airlines: how much could each airline's annual revenue grow by integrating Odys eVTOL aircraft at specific hub airports, based on real flight and catchment-area travel data?

How Did I Do This?

  1. 1. Framing the objective The question Odys and the airlines actually needed answered wasn't "would eVTOLs help" in the abstract — it was where specifically to deploy a limited number of aircraft first, so the analysis had to end in a ranked, hub-by-hub revenue estimate, not just a general feasibility case.
  2. 2. Catchment airport assignment Mapped each major hub (SEA, ORD, DTW, MSP, DFW, IAH) to its catchment airports — the smaller regional/connecting airports that realistically feed passenger volume into it — since an eVTOL's value comes from shortening the regional leg into a hub, not the long-haul leg out of it.
  3. 3. Filtering flyer types Split passenger data into point-to-point (P2P) vs. connecting (CE) flyers, because these two groups respond differently to a new short-hop option — a P2P flyer is a direct revenue opportunity for regional eVTOL service, while a connecting flyer's value depends on whether the eVTOL leg feeds a longer itinerary.
  4. 4. Revenue analysis by region Ran the revenue model separately for three hub groupings — Pacific Northwest (SEA-Tac for Delta), Midwest (DTW & MSP for Delta, ORD for American), and the Houston Triangle (DFW for American, IAH for United) — rather than one blended national number, since catchment density and existing route economics differ enough between regions that averaging them would have hidden the actual best-first-deployment answer.
  5. 5. Results The Midwest came back as the strongest opportunity for both airlines analyzed: Delta could grow annual revenue by an estimated 2.4% integrating eVTOLs at MSP and DTW, and American by 1.6% at ORD — driven by the high number of catchment airports feeding those specific hubs.
  6. 6. Supporting tools Built reusable helper functions (total revenue, driver counts, drivers organized by income group, most popular tracts) so the underlying model could be re-run for other hub/airline combinations beyond the three presented, rather than being a one-off calculation.
