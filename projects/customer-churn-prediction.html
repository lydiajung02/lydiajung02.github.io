How Did I Do This?

  1. 1. Data & cleaning Started from a telecom-style subscription dataset (tenure, contract type, monthly charges, support tickets, usage). Imputed missing tenure values with group medians and one-hot encoded categorical fields.
  2. 2. Exploratory analysis Plotted churn rate against tenure, contract type, and payment method to sanity-check which signals were worth keeping before modeling. Contract type and tenure showed the clearest separation.
  3. 3. Modeling Started with logistic regression as an interpretable baseline (AUC 0.81), then moved to XGBoost. Churn was imbalanced (~27% positive), so I used class weighting rather than naive oversampling to avoid inflating minority-class confidence, and tuned depth/learning rate with grid search + 5-fold CV.
  4. 4. Evaluation Optimized for recall on the highest-risk decile rather than raw accuracy — in this business, the cost of missing a churner is much higher than the cost of a wasted retention offer. Final model: 0.89 ROC-AUC, 78% recall on the top decile of predicted risk.
  5. 5. Business translation Converted model output into a 1–5 risk tier per customer and joined it back to monthly recurring revenue to estimate ~$340K in annual revenue sitting in the top two risk tiers — the number the retention team actually used to prioritize outreach.
